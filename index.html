FPGA Camera Vision Accelerator

VerilogFPGADigital DesignClock-Domain CrossingBRAMImage Processing

Hardware acceleration of OpenCV's ORB algorithm on Nexys A7-100T running at 1 pixel/clock @ 100 MHz; streaming live from OV7670 camera module to VGA output.

Read more...Show less

Problem / Inspiration: Primarily, I pursued this project because I wanted to introduce myself to hardware acceleration and I had an FPGA provided for my EE354 class.

Contributions: I first built out OV7670 Camera capture module with async FIFO for clock-domain crossing (25 MHz, 100 MHz). Added BRAM frame-buffering first to separate VGA output and later instantiating more for ORB. Then I built Sobel Edge Filtering with 3x3 window built from line buffers. Scaled up previous line buffers and sliding windows to implement pipelined FAST-9 corner detection and then worked on ORB descriptors alongside partner. Ran testbenches and simulations for individual module verification.

Results / Outcomes: Live 640x480 VGA output at 100 MHz with raw grayscale, sobel edge filtering, and FAST keypoints (See videos!) Design supports up to 384 ORB keypoints per frame. I got a lot of exposure into CV theory; learned more about pipelining in hardware. I want to later integrate ORB outputs with CPU in a more full robotics system hopefully.

Scrambler Humanoid Robot

JAXMuJoCoReinforcement LearningPPOComputer Vision

2nd Place Project at Himalaya Hacks; we explored how a Unitree G1 could better traverse harsh and steep environment (like Mt. Everest) utilizing passive grippers to "scramble".

Read more...Show less

Problem / Inspiration: Humanoids can travel max 30 degrees inclines by walking. We want to allow them to be able to travel steeper grades where they support rather than burden human expeditions.

Contributions: In 36 hours developed a two-stage PPO pipeline for the 29-DOF Unitree G1, targeting slopes of 30–45 degrees in simulation. Also built an ArUco-based vision pipeline that tracks passive compliant-hand deformation to estimate grip quality without any sensors/electronics in contact with extreme terrain.

Results / Outcomes: Won 2nd place at Himalaya Hacks judged by robotics & ML engineers as well as professional mountaineers! Got cool exposure to RL and made me want to take classes for it.

UNIT Fitness

Embedded SystemsiOS App DevFoundingBLEIMUSignal Processing

Retrofitted hardware for gym weight stack machines for automatic tracking of reps, sets, and effort. Started as EE105 final project, ended up competing in USC's MEPC startup incubator (11 selected of 103 startups)

Read more...Show less

Problem / Inspiration: I was inspired by research I had seen of how exercise velocity is a great measure of how close you are to muscular failure during a workout set; Wanted to apply this with something as cheap as a class-provided Arduino Nano.

Contributions: I led the technical development (both hardware + full-stack software) as CTO of UNIT Fitness as I wanted to build easy and cheap smart workout tracking for general bodybuilders. I developed algorithms to process IMU acceleration data (in z-direction) into filtered velocity data. Took the live streamed velocity data and was able to count reps in real time and even segment the concentric phase of each rep for further analysis. Finally re-implemented effort (or RIR) tracking for users. Integrated IMU processing with HX711 Loadcell data to store a validated weight lifted for exercises. When it was a class project, I took lead of my team for systems integration, sensor processing, front-end, ML training, and the presentation.

Results / Outcomes: Won Best Project for class, then spun off into startup with different team. Built working MVP of the entire sensor to app system (as seen in videos). Almost launched in USC Gym! Really happy I got startup/founding experience; got to do customer discovery and such. Ultimately realized I lean more towards being an engineer than founder. No longer working on this :(

ID 0: Agentic Hardware Robot

ROS 2Computer VisionAprilTags

Project at YC Robohacks built atop Innate hardware; we imagined an agentic robot that could autonomously design, print, and swap its end-effector/tool for various tasks.

Read more...Show less

Problem / Inspiration: Most robots are limited by the end-effectors they are designed with. A gripper meant for general manipulation will be terrible at turning screws. Furthermore, robots require human assistance in designing, manufacturing, and swapping tools for different use cases.

Contributions: Integrated existing Innate robot hardware + ROS stack with universal end-effector system. Implemented autonomous behavior for robot to "scan" an object, then begin generating and printing design for application-specific effector on the universal base. Utilized April Tags to locate and swap effectors after printing complete. This was my first experience with ROS 2, I worked mostly on the April Tag systems and assisted with the CAD generation.

Results / Outcomes: Built in 36 hours and presented at YC RoboHacks to founders, investors, and judges. Learned more about ROS 2 and to be fully understand robot systems before modifying/adding features.
